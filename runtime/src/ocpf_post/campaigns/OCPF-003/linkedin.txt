One thing writing about OpenClaw made very clear to me: a system is only useful if it can tell you what actually happened.

That applies to social automation too.

If a publishing job targets three platforms and one fails, the whole job should not collapse into one vague “failed” state.

The destinations that succeeded should stay successful.
The destination that failed should keep a truthful retry state.
The history should tell you exactly what happened.

That is the rule I am carrying into OneClickPostFactory.

“Post everywhere” is easy to demo.
Knowing which consequence happened, which did not, and what is safe to retry is what makes the workflow usable.
